The rest of the heat

    How the heat is scored

    Each listed stock in the window gets a recency-weighted buy mass: log of the high end of each official purchase range, decayed with a 10-day half-life. More unique buyers raise the score. Repeat tickets from one filer still count, but a cluster of independent buyers counts more. Net selling in the same window cuts it. A committee-overlap buy gets a lift. A burst versus the prior 3-year baseline gets a lift. Mega-caps get a slight haircut so a lonely mid-cap print can surface. Bonds, options, ETFs, preferreds, and private names are out — you cannot buy those on the tape.

    STOCK Act amounts are ranges, not share counts. Filings can lag the trade by weeks. Price change is from the close on the last buy date to the latest close on file — not proof anyone knew anything.
